A Song for Someone Who Matters and a Model Lesson

Two things are in this page: a song I made for someone who matters to me, and a short lesson about the three kinds of AI models you keep hearing about. By the end you should be able to look at a task and say which family fits it, and why — without hand-waving.

LLM

Reads and writes text. Predicts the next token.

Multimodal model

Takes in images, audio or video as well as text.

Diffusion model

Generates images, audio or video by undoing noise.

Family 1

LLMs: one token at a time

A large language model is a network trained to answer one narrow question over and over: given this sequence of tokens, what token probably comes next?

From text to numbers, and back

Text is first cut into tokens — chunks of a few characters or a short word. Each token becomes an ID, each ID becomes a vector (a list of numbers). Those vectors pass through many transformer layers. Inside each layer, self-attention lets every position look at the other positions and mix in their information; a small feed-forward network then transforms each position. Stack 30–100 of these layers and the final vector is turned into a score for every token in the vocabulary — that score distribution is the model's output.

How it is trained

Take a huge pile of text. Hide the next token, predict it, measure how wrong you were (cross-entropy loss), nudge every weight slightly in the direction that reduces the error, repeat a few trillion times. That is the whole training signal. Nobody hand-writes rules of grammar into it, and nobody loads a database of facts — the facts end up implicitly encoded in the weights.

How it generates

Predict → pick a token → append it → predict again. This loop is called autoregressive generation. The picking step is randomised a little: temperature scales the distribution, and top-p cuts off the unlikely tail. Raise the temperature and you get more surprising words; drop it to zero and the model becomes repetitive and flat.

What an LLM is not

Not a search engine

There is no lookup happening. When a model gets a fact right, it is because the pattern was learnable from the data; when it gets one wrong, it produces something equally fluent. That is hallucination, and it comes from the mechanism, not from a bug.

Not aware of “now”

Its knowledge stops at the training cut-off, and it has no senses. It can only see what is placed in its context window — the tokens you or an app hand it this time.

中文小结：大语言模型（LLM）本质上是“预测下一个 token”的网络。文字被切成 token、变成向量， 穿过很多层 Transformer（注意力机制让每个位置参考其他位置），输出是词表上的概率分布。训练信号只有一个：预测下一个词、 算误差、调参数。它没有数据库、没有检索，事实是隐式地“长”在权重里的，所以也会流畅地编造。
Stop here. Ask: “Why would this design make a confident wrong answer inevitable, not just possible?” Then let them play with the next-token demo. Point out that every click is a full forward pass through the network.
Family 2

Multimodal models: pictures become sequences too

A multimodal model accepts more than one modality — text, images, audio, video — instead of text alone. The trick is not that it “sees”. The trick is that it converts everything into the same kind of object a transformer already understands: a sequence of vectors.

An image is not fed in as a picture

The image is resized and cut into a grid of square patches (commonly 14×14 or 16×16 pixels). Each patch is flattened into numbers and passed through a vision encoder — usually a Vision Transformer, or a contrastively pre-trained encoder such as CLIP. Out comes one vector per patch. With position information added, those vectors form a sequence that can be concatenated with text token vectors and fed into the same transformer.

Many practical systems use a second variation: keep a pre-trained vision encoder, and train a small projection layer that maps its output into the language model's embedding space. The language model is then frozen or lightly tuned. Either way, the picture has become tokens.

Audio and video

Audio is turned into a mel-spectrogram or a stream of short frames; frames are then patched or tokenised exactly like an image, or encoded by an audio encoder (Whisper-style) and projected. Video is an ordered sequence of frames — order and timing carry real information, so models sample frames, add temporal position cues, or use attention across time. That ordering is why “video” is harder than “a bag of photos”.

Drag the slider to change the patch grid. The chips below show the first few patches reduced to a colour triple. A real vision encoder produces a rich vector per patch — hundreds or thousands of numbers carrying texture, edges and object parts. Collapsing each patch to its average colour just lets you see the step “picture → grid of vectors” with your own eyes.

What changes, and what does not

The reasoning core is usually still a language model working over a sequence. What changed is the front door: new encoders for new modalities, and a projection that puts them in a shared space. That is also why a multimodal model can be bad at reading a chart while being excellent at describing a photo — the encoder and the resolution decide what the language model ever gets to see.

中文小结：多模态模型不是“真的看见”，而是把图像切成小块（patch），每块经视觉编码器变成向量， 再投影到和文字 token 同一个向量空间里——于是图片也变成了“序列”。音频转频谱/帧后同样处理；视频则是有顺序的帧， 时间顺序本身携带信息。推理核心通常仍是语言模型，变的是入口的编码器。
Stop here. Ask: “If the model only ever receives a grid of vectors, what could it plausibly miss?” Good answers: small text, fine detail, anything cropped out by the resize, motion between sampled frames.
Family 3

Diffusion models: learning to undo noise

A diffusion model generates media by starting from pure noise and removing noise step by step. It is a completely different generation recipe from next-token prediction.

The idea in two directions

Forward (fixed, no learning): take a real image and add a little Gaussian noise. Repeat a few hundred or a thousand times. At the end you have static that contains no visible trace of the original — because the process is designed to destroy information gradually and predictably.

Reverse (this is what gets learned): train a network to look at a noisy image at step t and estimate the noise that was added. The loss is simply the squared error between the true noise and the predicted noise. Once that network is good, you can start from fresh noise and subtract its predictions step by step until an image appears that was never in the training set.

Where the text prompt enters

The network is also given an encoding of the prompt. It is injected through cross-attention, so at every denoising step the noise estimate is conditioned on what you asked for. Classifier-free guidance sharpens this: run the prediction with and without the prompt and extrapolate away from the unconditional result, trading diversity for prompt-adherence.

Why “latent” diffusion matters

Running diffusion directly on megapixel images is expensive. Latent diffusion first compresses the image with a variational autoencoder into a small latent grid (think 64×64×4 instead of 1024×1024×3), runs the whole denoising process there, and decodes back to pixels at the end. Almost every image generator you have used does the hard work in that compressed space.

Try it

Denoising, step by step

This is a simulation, not a real diffusion model. A target picture is blended with noise; the slider stands in for “denoising step”. The real thing predicts noise with a network at every one of ~20–1000 steps. What the simulation does show honestly is the shape of the process: coarse structure appears first, fine detail last.

What a diffusion model is not

It does not predict “the next pixel”. It refines a whole noisy tensor at once, and it produces the entire output in one coordinated process. That is why diffusion is the natural fit for continuous media — and why it is not used to write an essay.

中文小结：扩散模型学的是“去噪”。前向过程把真实图像一步步加噪到纯噪声（固定规则，不学习）； 反向过程训练一个网络在每一步预测并减去噪声，损失就是预测噪声和真实噪声的均方误差。文字提示通过交叉注意力注入， 再用无分类器引导增强贴合度。潜变量扩散先在 VAE 压缩出的小空间里做去噪，最后解码回像素。它不是“预测下一个像素”， 而是一次次整体精修。
Stop here. Ask: “Why does the model need hundreds of small steps instead of one big jump?” Then run the slider slowly together: coarse shapes at low steps, texture at high steps.
Putting it together

Same or different?

The three families are best separated by what they take in, what they put out, and how they generate — not by which product name they appear under.

Where the boundaries blur — this is the part worth remembering

A multimodal model usually contains an LLM

“Multimodal” describes the front door, not a separate species. Strip the encoders off and what is left is a language model reading a longer sequence.

Diffusion is not only for images

The same noising/denoising recipe works for audio waveforms and video frames. “Diffusion” names a generation method, not a medium.

Some image generators are autoregressive

If an image is tokenised into discrete codes, it can be generated with next-token prediction like text. So “image generator” does not automatically mean “diffusion”.

Real products are pipelines

A single app may have an LLM rewrite your prompt, then call a diffusion model to render it, then call another model to check the result. Three families, one button.

And the honest caveat: do not assume a specific product works the way this page describes. These are the three mainstream recipes. Companies mix them, and the internals of named products are often not public. When it matters, check the technical report — not the marketing page.

中文小结：区分三者要看清“输入 / 输出 / 生成方式”，而不是看产品名。LLM 是自回归地一个 token 一个 token 往外吐；多模态通常就是“编码器 + LLM”，把别的模态也变成 token 序列；扩散模型是整张图反复去噪。边界会模糊： 多模态模型里往往装着一个 LLM、扩散也能做音频视频、有些图像生成其实是自回归的、真实产品常常是三者的流水线。 不要假设某个具体产品的内部就是这一页描述的样子。
